Reinforcement Learning · Robotics · Simulation

A robotic arm that taught itself to draw.

RoboSketch-RL is a vision-guided reinforcement-learning system. No stroke was programmed by hand — a PPO agent learned to reproduce each shape through trial and error, scored by how well its drawing overlaps the target. Everything here was built and trained in simulation.

How it works

A closed loop: draw → see → score

On the real robot, ink on paper is invisible to code — the only way to know what was drawn is to look. So the system photographs the paper with a virtual camera and reconstructs the drawing with OpenCV. That reconstruction is what the reward is computed from.

The novelty · Hypothesis H2

Can it learn from a camera, not ground truth?

The real robot never knows the "true" drawing — ink on paper is invisible to code, so a camera is all it has. So we ran the key experiment: reward the agent only from what the camera perceives (top row = exact ground truth, bottom row = camera). Same everything else; both scored on the true drawing.

Example drawings — top: ground-truth reward, bottom: camera reward. Both rows produce recognizable shapes.

The rigorous version: true IoU, mean ± std over 3 seeds per bar. Ground truth 0.38 ±0.05, camera 0.35 ±0.06 — the bars overlap on every shape (vision better on the circle). Camera-reward and ground-truth-reward are statistically indistinguishable in drawing quality: the agent learns just as well from noisy perception as from perfect knowledge — the premise the whole project rests on.

Reward integrity · Hypothesis H3

Is the agent gaming the camera?n=1 per shape

A camera reward is a proxy for the real drawing, and the classic failure mode of optimising a proxy is a policy that earns perceived credit it never actually drew. So every episode of training was logged twice over — what the camera credited, and what was truly on the paper — and the gap between them tracked across 200k steps on each shape.

Verdict: the camera reward is not being gamed. Across 3328 episodes the mean gap is −0.0001, and 95% of all gaps fall within ±0.013. The two signs cancel — line and circle run slightly negative (the camera under-credits the agent), square and triangle slightly positive — so there is no systematic direction, which is what a proxy with no exploitable structure looks like. The ground-truth control, whose gap must be identically zero by construction, measured 0.000000000 over 832 episodes, so the logging is trustworthy before any vision run is read.

Limits, stated plainly: one seed per shape, so a small per-shape effect could hide inside the ±0.05 seed spread the H2 sweep showed. And this tests the fast proxy camera — a more realistic camera has more structure to exploit and must be re-tested.

Unexpected finding

The agents finish early, overshoot, then idlepreliminary · n=1 per cell

Recording IoU at every step of an evaluation, rather than only at the end, showed something no summary number could: the shape is finished almost immediately, the score then falls, and the remainder of the episode does nothing at all.

IoU against step for each cell. The curves are flat for most of the episode — the agent is not slowly degrading its drawing, it loses the IoU in a short burst just after completion and then stops changing the canvas.

Why it happens. The episode runs to max_steps=240, the pen is always down, and there is no terminate action. Having drawn the shape the agent cannot stop, cannot lift the pen and cannot end the episode. Standing perfectly still is technically reachable, but it is a single point in a continuous action space that a Gaussian policy will not land on exactly — so it drifts a little past the outline first, then settles.

What it costs. Two things. Every IoU this project has reported is measured after the overshoot, so the policies are better than their scores: peak IoU runs 0.355–0.578 against reported 0.319–0.505. And roughly 74% of every episode is spent on a canvas that never changes again — at 200k steps per run, about 150k steps of wasted sample budget.

Hypothesis, not a result — must not be quoted as one.

The camera arm loses about twice as much IoU to the overshoot as the ground-truth arm. Scored at their peak the two are level; scored at the end, ground truth appears 0.039 ahead — close to the whole 0.028 gap the H2 sweep reports. That raises a specific, testable worry: the small edge ground truth appears to hold in H2 may be an artifact of when we stop measuring. But these are the single-run 400k models, n=1 per cell, not the 200k × 3-seed runs the H2 table is built from. It would make the project's central claim stronger, which is exactly why it needs checking rather than adopting. The test — re-running the H2 sweep with peak IoU logged alongside final IoU — has not been done.

Fixes under test: a plateau-truncation switch and an explicit stop action are implemented and default-off, and an ablation across the three arms is currently running. No results from it are claimed here.

Ablation · O1

Does telling the agent how much time is left help?24 runs · 3 seeds

Nothing in the observation said how many steps remained, so two identical canvases with 5 versus 100 steps left looked the same to the policy while requiring different actions — the task was partially observable. O1 appends a constant-valued fourth channel carrying the remaining fraction of the episode. Both arms ran the identical protocol, same hyperparameters and same seeds, on ground-truth reward; only the flag differs.

Verdict: modest, and shape-dependent. About +0.04 IoU in 9 of 12 paired runs, sitting right at the conventional threshold of evidence — real enough to keep, too small to headline. Triangle (+0.073, all three seeds improved) and square (+0.064) carry it; line is marginal (+0.026) and circle is flat-to-negative (−0.008).

A prediction that did not survive. The stated reason for running this was that O1 should reduce the overshoot — the end of the episode is where the overshoot happens, and O1 tells the agent the end is coming. The data says no: mean overshoot change +0.001, 5 of 12 pairs, t = 0.11. Completely flat. Whatever O1 is doing, it is not fixing the overrun. Nor does it reduce wandering: path_ratio moved the wrong way on balance.

No variance claim. Seed spread narrowed on square and triangle but widened on line and circle — two up, two down, nothing to claim. Decision: keep the flag, leave the default off. It changes the observation shape, invalidating every saved 3-channel model, and a +0.04 effect does not justify breaking compatibility while the overrun work — worth 0.064, and possibly interacting with it — is unsettled.

Known caveats

Measurement caveats, current as of 3 Sep 2026

  • Every reported IoU is measured after the overshoot. Scores are taken at the final step, which is 4–8 steps past the agent's peak. Peak IoU runs 0.355–0.578 against reported 0.319–0.505, and the camera arm loses about twice as much as the ground-truth arm — so the headline IoUs understate the policies, and possibly unevenly between the two arms of H2.
  • eval_eps=8 is a no-op: it evaluates the same episode eight times. The evaluation is fully deterministic — the pen start is the target pixel nearest the paper centre rather than an RNG draw, and prediction is deterministic. The eight episodes produce eight pixel-identical canvases and an IoU std of 0.000000000, so there is no per-episode variation in any of these numbers.
  • The ±std reported for H2 is sound. It comes from the 3 training seeds, which genuinely differ — not from the eight identical evaluation episodes. What would be wrong is any claim of the form “averaged over 8 episodes”. The fix is either eval_eps=1 or real evaluation variation; neither is applied yet.
  • H3 and the overrun table are n=1. H3 ran one seed per shape (3328 episodes, but a single training run each); the per-step overrun table comes from the single-run 400k models in models/, one cell per shape×reward. Only H2 and O1 are multi-seed.
  • The overshoot ablation is still running. The plateau-truncation and stop-action arms are implemented and their sweep is in progress. Nothing on this page reports its outcome.

See it yourself

Run any of it from a terminal

From the project folder, in the robosketch environment:

# draw a shape yourself with the pen
conda run -n robosketch python sim/05_interactive_draw.py

# watch the arm draw, then the camera score it
conda run -n robosketch python sim/04_camera_capture.py square

# watch an agent learn (short run, ~2 min)
conda run -n robosketch python rl/train.py --shape circle --timesteps 40000
